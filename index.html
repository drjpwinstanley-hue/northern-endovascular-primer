Northern Endovascular Primer

Welcome to the Northern Endovascular Primer, a virtual reality home simulation programme for early-years endovascular trainees. This course has been designed to gain familiarity with the basic steps of peripheral angiography using the Mentice VIST®G5 simulator.

You will have an introductory session with your mentor and then use of the home simulator over a two month period. You are expected to progress through each of the cases in turn and will have a follow-up session with an assessor at the end of your course. As a minimum we expect you to be able to perform a diagnostic angiogram and understand the basic steps of treating stenotic disease by the end of this period.

This is a great opportunity to learn some of the basic steps and gain confidence before you transfer your skills to vascular patients. Very few trainees in the country have access to this level of simulation training, please make the most of it!

Introduction to procedures.

This video gives details about the equipment you will need to carry out procedures. This includes wires and catheters.

In medical practice, the thickness of wires is measured in fractions of an inch. The commonest sizes are 0.035 inch, 0.018 inch and 0.014 inch. Commonly 0.035 wires are used in larger vessels such as the aortoiliac segment or superficial femoral artery (SFA). In the femoropopliteal segment we are likely to use 0.018 wires and the smallest 0.014 wires are commonly used in distal tibial work. It is important to note that the sensors in the simulator are sensitive to the different wire thicknesses so if you select an 0.035 wire on the laptop you need to insert an 0.035 wire otherwise it will not appear on screen. Likewise, if you select a 5Fr catheter, it will only appear if you insert the diameter. All the catheters and wires provided are straight but they can simulate lots of different shapes if you select a directional catheter in the scenario (e.g. cobra, SOS…).

Ipsilateral iliac stenosis.

Here you will start to perform balloon angioplasty for a common iliac stenosis. You will need to cross the stenosis and perform diagnostic angio runs before performin angioplasty. Work on optimising the image quality and feel free to run the scenario several times experimenting with different wires to cross the stenosis and different sizes of balloon.

Antegrade femoral angioplasty.

Here you will perform an SFA angioplasty via an antegrade approach. The anatomy loaded into the simulator doesn’t allow full diagnostic runs down to the foot but focus on treating the femoropopliteal segment.

8

Cross bifurcation iliac angioplasty.

The previous iliac lesion was treated by an ipsilateral retrograde common femoral artery puncture. Here we use a cross-bifurcation approach to treat the contralateral common iliac artery. This will give you practice at crossing the aortic bifurcation which is a commonly required skill. There are lots of different catheters you can use for this task but a catheter with a recurrent curve or a shepherd’s crook shape will be most appropriate.
